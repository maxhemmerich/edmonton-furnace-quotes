First, the right request

A furnace that still heats is a repair. The form treats it differently from a replacement.

The form is short, but what you pick in it decides how the request is read. A furnace sent in for replacement gets priced as a new furnace — the largest bill a homeowner pays — when the fault may only be a part. Send it in as the job it actually is and you get a repair price to compare.

  1. Job: pick “Furnace repair”

    In the “What do you need?” menu. “Furnace repair” is for a furnace that still heats the house and is misbehaving; “No heat — urgent” is for one that has stopped heating it altogether.

  2. Timing: say what is actually true

    The “How soon?” menu tells the contractor whether the furnace is still keeping the house warm while you wait, or the house is cold and this is a today job. If the house is cold, that is a no-heat request and this site routes those first.

  3. Description: what it is doing, and how old it is

    The description box is what a contractor reads before deciding whether to call. Say what the furnace actually does — short-cycles every few minutes, an error code on the panel and the code itself, one room that never warms up, a bang or a rumble on startup — how much of the house is warm, and the make and rough age if you know them. If a technician has already looked at it, put what they said in your own words.

For example: “Furnace still heats, but it short-cycles every few minutes and the panel shows an error code. Two-storey house, the upstairs never gets warm. Mid-2000s unit. Looking for a repair quote before I decide anything.” That kind of detail is what gets a real number instead of a call back to ask questions you could have answered in the box.

What to ask for

What a written repair quote should contain.

We do not do the work and we do not quote for it, so there is no price on this site. A licensed contractor quotes you. These are the things worth having on paper before you agree to anything:

  • The price, in writing, before work starts. Ask for it by email so there is a record of what was quoted, not just what was said on the phone.
  • Whether the diagnostic or trip charge is separate. Many contractors charge to come out and diagnose before any repair is agreed. Ask what that charge is, and whether it comes off the repair price if you go ahead.
  • What the part is, and why it is the one being replaced. Ask for the part named in writing, so a second opinion can tell whether the same fault was found twice.
  • What is covered after the repair, and for how long. Ask whether the part and the labour carry any guarantee, and for how long. Know it before you agree, not after.
  • The contractor's Alberta licence. Ask for the licence number and check the contractor's standing before you sign. A licensed contractor hands it over without blinking.
Being straight with you: nothing on this site is a price, a ranking or a recommendation. No contractor is named, rated or reviewed here, and we do not take a cut of your repair bill. Contractors pay us a flat price if they choose to receive requests — that is the whole business. And if a technician tells you the repair is close to the price of a new furnace, the replacement page sets out what that quote should contain.

What happens after you send it

It reaches contractors who work in your postal code. They call you with a price.

  1. You send the request

    Name, phone, postal code and what the furnace is doing. That is the whole form. It has no server — it builds your request and opens it in your own email app, and nothing leaves your browser until you press Send there.

  2. It goes to heating contractors who work in your part of Edmonton

    The postal code you give decides which side of the city that is. Not a national call centre, and not a blast to fifteen companies.

  3. You get called with a price

    Expect a call or a text. Ask for the price in writing, and ask the questions above before you agree. You owe nobody anything for the quote.

Being straight with you: this is a new quote-request service in Edmonton, started in October 2026. If we cannot route your request to a contractor who can quote the job, we will email you and say so, rather than leave you waiting on it.

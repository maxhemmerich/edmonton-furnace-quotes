Replacing a furnace in Alberta? Here is what a government program can still pay.

You asked the right question before you signed: what can a furnace replacement get back in money? Below is every federal and Alberta program that touches a furnace or a heat pump, what the government's own page says about it, and the date we read that page. Two things stand out. Most of the money you see advertised online is from programs that have already closed. And the one large grant that is still being paid out to approved applicants does not apply to a home heated with natural gas.

First, the short answer

For a natural-gas furnace replacement in Edmonton there is no open, general-purpose rebate that pays cash in 2026.

That is the honest headline, and it is not the answer most pages give you. Three reasons it is true, each from the sources set out below:

  • The federal cash grants are closed. The Canada Greener Homes Grant — the one that paid up to $5,000 — stopped taking applications, and the interest-free Canada Greener Homes Loan closed to new applications as well. A page still quoting those figures in 2026 is quoting a closed program.
  • The one big grant still being paid is for oil-heated homes, not gas. The Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program paid up to $10,000, and it stopped taking applications on July 31, 2026 — and it lists natural gas as an ineligible heating fuel.
  • Alberta's provincial tool is financing, not a rebate. The Clean Energy Improvement Program lends you money that you repay on your property tax bill. In the City of Edmonton it does not cover a high-efficiency gas furnace unless that furnace is installed together with a heat pump.
None of that stops the request. A furnace still gets replaced, and a licensed contractor still quotes it. The only question this page settles is whether a program pays part of it back — and for a plain gas-for-gas replacement in Edmonton, in 2026, the answer from the government's own pages is no.

Alberta programs

Alberta: financing, not a rebate — and in Edmonton a gas furnace is not covered on its own.

Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP)

CEIP is the province's main home-energy instrument. It is administered by Alberta Municipalities and delivered through participating municipalities, including the City of Edmonton. It is a loan tied to your property tax bill, not a grant. On the City of Edmonton's own program page:

  • “Residential property owners and owners of multi-unit residential buildings (MURBs) can finance a minimum of $3,000 and up to $50,000 of retrofits through the program.”
  • Edmonton's own eligibility list puts a “High-efficiency gas furnace (unless combined with a heat pump)” in the not eligible column. So a gas furnace on its own cannot be financed here — only a furnace installed together with a heat pump.
  • Repayment is added to your property tax bill, and the balance stays with the property if you sell. This is money you borrow and pay back, not money you get back.

Source: ceip.abmunis.ca/residential/locations/edmonton, read 9 Oct 2026. On the “not a rebate” point, the City of Calgary's program page puts it plainly: “CEIP is not a rebate program. You must pay back all of the funding that you borrow.” (calgary.ca — Residential CEIP, read 9 Oct 2026.) Each municipality sets its own terms, rates and intake status; confirm Edmonton's before you rely on it.

Alberta Energy Rebate

If you have seen an “Alberta rebate” advertised in 2026, this is almost certainly the one — and it has nothing to do with a furnace. The Government of Alberta's page describes it as a one-time payment introduced “to help with the cost of groceries, fuel and other everyday essentials”, and it is a flat $100 for an eligible Alberta resident. It is a cost-of-living payment, not an efficiency grant, and it does not change whether you replace a furnace or not. Source: alberta.ca/alberta-energy-rebate, read 9 Oct 2026.

So the honest Alberta picture, on the sources above: no province-wide cash rebate for a furnace or a heat pump, one financing program that in Edmonton does not cover a gas furnace by itself, and a $100 cost-of-living rebate that is not an efficiency program at all.

What to do with this

Two things to settle before you sign a replacement quote.

  • Ask whether the contractor is proposing a heat pump instead of, or alongside, the furnace. On the figures above, fuel switching is the only path a current or recent federal grant is written for, and the only path Edmonton's CEIP financing covers for a gas furnace. If you are staying on gas, budget as though no program is coming.
  • Ask for every rebate the contractor mentions to be named on paper with the program's own web page. A figure with no live government page behind it is a talking point, not a rebate. The pages above are the ones to check it against.
  • Keep the money-back question and the price question separate. What a replacement costs is between you and a licensed contractor, and this site carries no price because we do not do the work. This page is only about the part a program might pay back.

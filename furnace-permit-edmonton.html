Edmonton, Alberta · Replacing a furnace · Permits

Replacing a furnace in Edmonton needs a permit. Here is what the City's own pages say.

You are about to pay for a furnace, and the paperwork is part of the job. Two City permits can sit beside a furnace replacement: a gas permit, required under the provincial Safety Codes Act, and a heating and ventilating permit for the heating work. Below is what the City of Edmonton's own pages require — each line quoted from the page named beside it, with the date we read it — and what it is worth having a contractor put in the written quote.

First, the short answer

Yes. The City's own page names furnaces explicitly, and the permit is applied for before the work starts.

Three things follow from the City's pages, and none of them is our opinion:

  • A gas permit covers gas equipment, and the City lists furnaces first. The permit is required under the provincial Safety Codes Act for installing, altering or relocating that equipment — a furnace replacement is squarely inside it.
  • The permit comes before the work, not after. The City's page says a permit must be applied for and issued prior to starting any work and requesting an inspection — so a quote that starts the job first is a quote with a problem in it.
  • A homeowner can hold a permit, but only inside limits. The homeowner permit is for renovation or re-testing of an existing gas system in a single detached house, and a homeowner may not install a new gas line — that is contractor work.
None of that stops the request. A furnace still gets replaced, and a licensed contractor still does the work and pulls the permit. This page is only about the paperwork that sits beside the job — so that the permit is a line you asked about, not a surprise after the furnace is in.

The City of Edmonton's own pages

What each page requires today.

Each entry below is a quotation from the City of Edmonton page named underneath it, followed by the date that page was read. Nothing here is paraphrased into a rule the City does not state, and no figure appears that is not on the page cited.

Gas permit — required, and a furnace is named in it

“A gas permit is required under the provincial Safety Codes Act when installing, altering or relocating gas equipment such as: furnaces, water heaters, meters, piping and lines, dryers, BBQs, garage heaters, ranges, patio heaters, fireplaces, space heaters, and fire pits.”

What that means: a replacement furnace is gas equipment, so the permit requirement covers it. The work is done by a licensed contractor, and the City's page notes contractors must be licensed with the City of Edmonton.

Source: https://www.edmonton.ca/residential_neighbourhoods/gas-permit (Gas Permits — Residential, City of Edmonton), read 9 Oct 2026.

Who may hold it — and the limit on a homeowner's own permit

“A homeowner may not install a new gas line. Only a qualified contractor may do the work.”

“A homeowner gas permit is for renovations and or re-test of an existing gas system in a single detached house only.”

What that means: if the job adds or moves a gas line, it is contractor work. If you are a homeowner in a single detached house doing limited renovation or re-test work yourself, the City states the bound on your own permit.

Source: https://www.edmonton.ca/residential_neighbourhoods/gas-permit, read 9 Oct 2026.

Before work starts

“A permit must be applied for and issued prior to starting any work and requesting an inspection.”

What that means: the sequence is permit first, then work. Starting without it is the part that turns a paperwork gap into a problem with the City.

Source: https://www.edmonton.ca/residential_neighbourhoods/gas-permit, read 9 Oct 2026.

Heating and ventilating permit — required for the heating work, with the City's own exception

“A heating and ventilating permit is required under the Safety Codes Permit Bylaw when installing, repairing, or altering any heating, ventilation, or air conditioning undertaking such as but not limited to” ventilating systems, kitchen exhaust, residential hydronic systems and heat recovery ventilators.

The City states one exception plainly: “A heating and ventilating permit is not required for: Installation of a gas-fired or solid-fuel-burning appliance, unless there is duct work attached to the appliance other than the combustion air duct.” In that case the City's page points to a Home Improvement Permit.

What that means: a straight swap where no duct work changes is the gas permit's job; if duct work is attached to the appliance, the City's own exception no longer applies, and a heating and ventilating permit enters the picture. Ask which one your job is.

Source: https://www.edmonton.ca/residential_neighbourhoods/heating-ventilation-permit (Heating and Ventilating Permits — Residential, City of Edmonton), read 9 Oct 2026.

Before the heating work starts

“A permit must be obtained before starting any work and booking an inspection.”

Source: https://www.edmonton.ca/residential_neighbourhoods/heating-ventilation-permit, read 9 Oct 2026.

What is deliberately not on this page: a permit fee. The City publishes its own permit fee schedule, and it changes. A fee that sat here as a number would be a figure we are not in a position to keep true, so it is not here — the City's page is. The fee is a charge from the City, not a charge from us, and this site carries no price at all: a licensed contractor quotes the work.

What to do with this

Two things to put in the quote conversation before you sign.

  • Ask whether the permit is included, and who pulls it. The price and the permit can be two different line items. Get on paper whether the permit is in the quote, and whether it is the contractor or you who applies for it. The request page makes the same point in its replacement checklist.
  • Ask for the permit, once it is pulled. The City requires the permit before the work and an inspection after it. Ask the contractor for the permit number, and ask whether the inspection is booked as part of the job — the City's pages put both on the contractor's side of the line, not yours.
  • Keep the permit question and the price question apart. This page is only about the City's paperwork. What a replacement costs is between you and a licensed contractor, and this site states no price because we do not do the work.

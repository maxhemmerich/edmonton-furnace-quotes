First, the timing

A tune-up is a fall job in Edmonton, not a January one.

Furnace maintenance is seasonal work. In Edmonton most of it is booked ahead of winter, while a visit can still be planned, rather than during the first cold snap when the same call becomes an emergency. That is the only reason to send the request early — the request is free and carries no obligation, so there is nothing to lose by asking sooner.

If the furnace is not heating at all, this is not the page. A tune-up is a scheduled maintenance visit. A house with no heat is an urgent repair and goes through the no-heat page; a furnace that still heats but is misbehaving is the repair request.

What to ask for

What to ask a contractor to check and record on an annual tune-up.

We do not service furnaces and we do not give technical advice on yours — a licensed contractor does the work. These are the things worth having on paper when you book a tune-up or annual maintenance visit:

  • What the visit covers, in writing. Ask what is included — what gets inspected, cleaned or tested, and what is charged separately. Two “tune-up” prices only compare if you know what each one includes.
  • Whether a written record of the visit comes with it. A maintenance record with the date, what was checked, what was found and what was changed is worth keeping — it is the history a later repair or replacement quote gets judged against.
  • Whether the visit includes the safety checks. Ask whether it covers the furnace's venting and its own safety devices. If you have a carbon-monoxide alarm, keep it; a tune-up does not replace one.
  • The contractor's Alberta licence. Ask for the licence number and check the contractor's standing before you book. A licensed contractor hands it over without blinking.
Being straight with you: nothing on this site is a price, a ranking or a recommendation. No contractor is named, rated or reviewed here, and we do not take a cut of your maintenance bill. We do not do the work ourselves — a licensed contractor visits and gives you the price.

What happens after you send it

It reaches contractors who work in your postal code. They call you with a price.

  1. You send the request

    Name, phone, postal code and what the furnace is doing. That is the whole form. It has no server — it builds your request and opens it in your own email app, and nothing leaves your browser until you press Send there.

  2. It goes to heating contractors who work in your part of Edmonton

    The postal code you give decides which side of the city that is. Not a national call centre, and not a blast to fifteen companies.

  3. You get called with a price

    Expect a call or a text. Ask for the visit and the price in writing, and ask the questions above before you book. You owe nobody anything for the quote.

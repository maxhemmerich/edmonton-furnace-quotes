First, the right request

A replacement is not a repair. The form treats them differently.

The form is short, but what you pick in it decides how the request is read. A replacement sent in as a repair gets priced like a repair — a part and a visit — and you are back where you started.

  1. Job: pick “Furnace replacement”

    In the “What do you need?” menu. “Furnace repair” is for a furnace that still heats the house and needs a part — that request is set out here; “Furnace replacement” is for one you have decided to change out.

  2. Timing: say what is actually true

    The “How soon?” menu tells the contractor whether this is a today job or a planned one. A replacement is usually planned, but if the house is cold, say so — that is a no-heat job and this site routes those first.

  3. Description: what it is doing, and how old it is

    The description box is what a contractor reads before deciding whether to call. Say whether the furnace still heats the house, how much of the house is cold, and the make and rough age if you know them. If a technician has already looked at it and told you what it needs, put that in your own words.

For example: “Furnace is a mid-1990s unit and still heats, but the repair quote came back close to the price of a new one. Two-storey house, 1,900 sq ft, no cold spots. Looking for a replacement quote.” That kind of detail is what gets a real number instead of a call back to ask questions you could have answered in the box.

What to ask for

What a written replacement quote should contain.

We do not do the work and we do not quote for it, so there is no price on this site. A licensed contractor quotes you. These are the things worth having on paper before you agree to anything:

  • The price, in writing, before work starts. Ask for it by email so there is a record of what was quoted, not just what was said on the phone.
  • Whether the permit is included. Ask whether the price covers the permit for the installation, and who pulls it. What the City of Edmonton requires →
  • Whether removal and disposal of the old furnace is included. It is a separate line on some quotes and built into others — either way, know which one you are looking at.
  • What make, model and size is being quoted. Two prices only compare if you know what equipment each one is for.
  • The contractor's Alberta licence. Ask for the licence number and check the contractor's standing before you sign. A licensed contractor hands it over without blinking.
Being straight with you: nothing on this site is a price, a ranking or a recommendation. No contractor is named, rated or reviewed here, and we do not take a cut of your installation bill. Contractors pay us a flat price if they choose to receive requests — that is the whole business.

What happens after you send it

It reaches contractors who work in your postal code. They call you with a price.

  1. You send the request

    Name, phone, postal code and what the furnace is doing. That is the whole form. It has no server — it builds your request and opens it in your own email app, and nothing leaves your browser until you press Send there.

  2. It goes to heating contractors who work in your part of Edmonton

    The postal code you give decides which side of the city that is. Not a national call centre, and not a blast to fifteen companies.

  3. You get called with a price

    Expect a call or a text. Ask for the price in writing, and ask the questions above before you agree. You owe nobody anything for the quote.

Being straight with you: this is a new quote-request service in Edmonton, started in October 2026. If we cannot route your request to a contractor who can quote the job, we will email you and say so, rather than leave you waiting on it.

Make it read as urgent

No-heat requests are routed first. Three fields make yours one of them.

The form is short, but what you pick in it decides how the request is treated. A no-heat job sent in as a tune-up sits behind the urgent ones.

  1. Job: pick “No heat — urgent”

    In the “What do you need?” menu. “Furnace repair” is for a furnace that still heats the house; “No heat — urgent” is for one that does not.

  2. Timing: “As soon as possible”

    The “How soon?” menu tells the contractor whether this is a today job or a next-month job. No heat is a today job.

  3. Description: say no heat, and say what it is doing

    The description box is what a contractor reads before deciding whether to call. Say how much of the house is cold, whether the fan runs, and any error code the furnace shows. Make and rough age help if you know them.

For example: “No heat anywhere in the house since this morning. Fan runs but the air is cold. Furnace is a mid-2000s unit, no error code on the panel.” That kind of detail is what gets a real price instead of a call back to ask questions you could have answered in the box.

What happens after you send it

It reaches contractors who work in your postal code. They call you with a price.

  1. You send the request

    Name, phone, postal code and what the furnace is doing. That is the whole form. It has no server — it builds your request and opens it in your own email app, and nothing leaves your browser until you press Send there.

  2. It goes to heating contractors who work in your part of Edmonton

    The postal code you give decides which side of the city that is. Not a national call centre, and not a blast to fifteen companies.

  3. You get called with a price

    Expect a call or a text. Ask for the price in writing, and check whether permits and the old furnace removal are in it before you agree. You owe nobody anything for the quote.

Where this covers

Edmonton, plus the metro codes beside it.

Coverage is set by your postal code, not your address. The form accepts Edmonton (T5A–T6X) plus T7X (Spruce Grove), T8A and T8B (Sherwood Park), T8N (St. Albert) and T9E (Leduc and Nisku). A code outside that set is refused at the postal field, with the reason, rather than taking a request that cannot be sent to a contractor who works there.
